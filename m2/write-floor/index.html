Scope and method¶

Each run is scored on the 14,000 held-out contexts of ex-2.2.21, under the full edit, which takes the e₁ component out of the state at every position and every slice.

We are fairly sure of the split under an absolute tolerance. It holds for any absolute tolerance above the largest excessExcess The write less the bound, in degrees. Positive when the blocks after the edit put more e₁ on the other ops than the clean model has there. at a symbol, 0.8°, and the two kinds are apart in bound on the left of the E3 figure, though their excesses overlap a little below the absolute tolerance. A rule relative to each site would draw the line elsewhere, flagging a few symbol sites whose small excess is unusual for that site. Which of the two a gate wants is a choice about what the relative tolerance is for.

We are less sure of the exact absolute tolerance. It rests on eight runs per recipe, and on the choice of the 95% quantile and the cut at 10°. It also rests on taking the quantile over the symbol and operand sites together. The operand sites alone would put it a few tenths of a degree higher (E2), and a few more sites would fall under it. A reference direction, edited at the same sites, would give a second scale for it that this report could not measure from stored data.

If the absolute tolerance were adopted, the reports that quote the relative tolerance would count one site over both on the recipe of record and 4 on falloff, all at operands of a single run on each recipe. They would also count sites on every run of pool 1–3, soft τ. The pool would then stand apart on the write as it already does on spillSpill The side effect of an edit on the other ops: the largest drop in the score of any op other than the one removed, net of what the same edit does to a control trained without the anchor. The selectivity criterion of D2.2 allows at most 0.02 at any dose., at the same slice.
