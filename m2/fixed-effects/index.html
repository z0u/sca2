The two recipes ran at different seeds, so we compare them as two samples of eight runs: the difference of the seed means, with a 95% Welch t interval, which does not assume the two recipes vary alike. The intervals are wide, since the end of a run is sensitive to small changes early in training (the fixed confirmation). With eight runs a side, a difference whose interval only just clears zero is suggestive rather than settled.

Removal and spill by dose (E1)¶

A smaller dose takes a share of e₁ out rather than all of it. We expect removalRemoval The drop in expected exact match on difference under the edit, as a share of the way from the clean score to the target null. Higher is better. to grow with the dose, and spill to stay low at every dose, as it did on falloff.

Removal grows with the dose on every run, from zero at dose 0, which is the clean pass. It rises slowly at small doses: the first quarter of the edit removes 29% of what the full edit does (14% to 40% by run), and the second quarter much more. By three quarters of the full edit, it has done all that the full edit does: on the seed mean, removal at that dose already matches removal at the full dose. Past three quarters it moves by no more than 0.02 either way on any run.

falloff has the same shape. On the seed mean it is a little lower at the smaller doses, by less than two sets of eight runs can tell apart: at half the edit, τ_ℓ fixed removes 0.07 (95% interval −0.06 to 0.20) more.

The slow rise at small doses is not in how far the edit turns the states at slice 1. The dose is the share of the e₁ component taken out of each state before it is put back on the sphere, so how far a dose turns a state depends on how close the state is to e₁. At slices 2 to 4 the states holding difference sit nearly on e₁ (median |αAlignment How far a state points along e₁: the cosine between the state and e₁, which for a unit-length state is its component along e₁; 1 on the anchor direction and 0 off it.| of 0.78, 0.98 and 0.75), where a partial dose turns a state far less than its share, and putting the state back on the sphere gives most of the alignment back: at slice 3, three quarters of the edit leaves 0.81 of the clean alignment. So at a single slice the turn is back-loaded, with most of it in the last quarter of the edit, where the measured removal has already finished. The bend has to come from how the slices compound: the edit at slice 1 starves what arrives at the later slices, which the edit there cuts by the same share again.

Spill is below zero at every dose on 7 runs of 8: the other ops lose less under the edit along e₁ than under the same edit along a typical other axis. The largest spill of any run at any dose is 0.003, on seed 717 (value-hsv, at the full dose), far below the criterion.

So the edit is selective at every dose, and the full edit is past the point where more dose removes more.
