This study measures all three on the eight falloff runs of split-anti-seeds, the experiment that set the current recipe of record. It uses the dose scoring that experiment stored, plus one new pass over the held-out contexts. That pass records the alignment with e₁ at every slice and position, clean and under the edit.

Scope¶

This is an exploratory re-analysis, with no preregistration and no gate. It covers the falloff arm of split-anti-seeds (the recipe of record) at model seeds 704 to 711. It also covers the control at each of those seeds from ex-2.2.23, which was trained without the anchor at the same initialization, batches, and label draws.

Every measurement is on the 14,000 held-out contexts of ex-2.2.21, 2,000 per op, each with three examples and a query. The edit is the full edit of the recipe: the whole e₁ component taken out of the state at every position and every slice. E2 and E3 use the full dose only, since split-anti-seeds already showed that the removal grows with the dose.

With eight runs, a pattern seen on every seed is one we can lean on; a difference between seeds is noted and not explained. E3 confirms on the recipe of record what ex-2.2.22 found informally on an earlier recipe, and takes it one step further.

How far the edit turns the other ops (E1)¶

We measure the turnWrite How far the edit turns a state: arcsin |α| of the alignment the state arrives with, taken at the 99th percentile over a group of states. At the embedding that is the clean α. It is taken before the edit at that slice, which leaves no e₁ behind. on the contexts of the other ops, since that is where a turn is a side effect. On difference contexts the edit is meant to turn the states, and E2 and E3 score what that does.

The anti-subspace term is meant to keep the states of other ops off e₁, so we expect the edit to turn them only a little. We expect the turn to be largest at slice 1, where split-anti-seeds found the other ops closest to e₁ at the end of training. It should be smaller at slices 2 to 4, where they sat lower. Those are the slices the pull acts on, and they are also deeper; the control sits a little lower with depth too, so this study cannot say which matters.

At slice 1 the write is about the bound. Only the embedding has been edited before it, and the embedding holds little. From slice 2 on, the other ops arrive with much less e₁ than the clean model gives them. The only difference from the clean pass is the edits at the slices before, so the drop comes from those. In this model each block moves the state a quarter of the way toward the output of its attention and again toward its MLP, so about half of the incoming state carries through. On the clean pass that carried part brings its e₁ along; under the edit it has none, and what arrives is only what the block adds. At the example answers that do not fit, the write is 39% of the bound at slice 2 and 18% at slice 4.

That is below the control too, which has no anchor. From slice 2 on, the write at those answers is 11° to 4°, against 15° to 10° on the clean pass of the control. So the blocks after an edit do not put e₁ back on the other ops; they leave less there than either model has without the edit.

The bound is widest at the answers of the other ops. At the example answers it is widest at slice 1, as we expected: there the answers of the other ops come partway onto e₁, about twice as far as in the control. A context whose examples all fit difference should lose most, and one whose examples point elsewhere should lose little. If the anchor holds the inferred op, the edit should take about the same share of the way to the target null in every group of contexts, whatever their posterior on difference. The posterior is what the examples say about the op, so an anchor that holds the posterior itself predicts the same; this test treats the two as one reading.

If the anchor holds something coarser, such as the label or a color feature of difference answers, the edit would take off about the same amount everywhere. That would be a larger share on the contexts that barely needed the op.

On the contexts of other ops, the edit helps where their examples point at difference, and the more they point at it, the more it helps. That is the direction the target null predicts, and the edit goes most of the way. It falls furthest short where the examples are most in doubt between difference and the true op.

Where the posterior on difference is ≥ 0.5, the edit gains 0.23 against 0.33 for the target null. In the two low bins it gains a little more than the target null, 0.08 against 0.05 at 0.05–0.2.

Part of the excess in the low bins comes from subtracting the control, which loses a little there under the edit. Where the examples point most at difference the control gains a little instead, on every seed, so there the net gain is the smaller one. Without subtracting the control, the gains are 0.05 at 0.05–0.2 and 0.26 at ≥ 0.5. Most contexts of other ops have almost no posterior on difference (10,180 of 12,000 below 0.05), and there the target null predicts almost no change.

So the drop is graded the way claim (b) asks: in proportion to what each context needed. The gain on the other ops fits the anchor holding what the examples show about difference, wherever it appears, rather than what the label marks.

Where is the rest? It could be on the answers of difference that the edit leaves behind, on colors no op gives, or on the answers of the other ops, weighed differently from the target null. So the shortfall is context by context. It could be in how the edited model weighs the ops, or in how it shapes the answer of each op. To separate the two, we fit each edited answer distribution with the nearest mixture of the answer distributions of the ops, with non-negative weights.

The edited answers are close to a mixture of the answers of the ops (0.11 (0.10 to 0.11), against 0.04 on the clean pass). So they are mostly well formed: answers the other ops give, with about the right spread within each op.

The mixture, though, is 0.36 (0.33 to 0.38) from the target null, so the edited model weighs the other ops differently from the posterior, context by context. In all, the distance left is about 2.4× the reference.

The difference in weights is not a systematic one. Weighing the other ops more evenly than the target null, or more sharply, moves the prediction further from the edited answers: with the weights raised to a power of 0.5 or 2 and renormalized, the distance is 0.43 or 0.42, against 0.39 for the target null itself. With the overall proportions about right as well, what is left looks like scatter from context to context.

The edited model does use the examples to weigh the other ops, partly. Take a predictor that has lost difference and weighs the other ops equally, whatever the examples show. It is further from the edited answers (0.53) than the target null is (0.39).

Claim (c) asks that the edited model answer as the target null does. This fits the reading of it in the design doc, that the anchor gives the edit a direction and not the whole distance. The edit takes difference away cleanly, and the model answers with the other ops. It weighs them by the examples, but less accurately than the target null weighs them.

Discussion¶

On the recipe of record, the edit behaves the way the anchor says it should on all three claims, to a first approximation. That fits the anchor holding the evidence for the op, wherever it appears, and the edit removing that evidence. In this grammar the op seems to be carried that way, spread over the example answers in the residual stream rather than held at one token. Whether part of it also lives in the attention, in what the heads match on (their query and key spaces) or what they carry (their value space), is open; we have put it in the backlog.

The shortfall is in where the answers land. The edit sends the mass to the right ops overall. But within a context, it shares the mass among them only partly by how well each fits the examples.

One reading is that the evidence the model uses to tell the other ops apart also travels partly along e₁, so taking out the axis blurs it. That would fit the anchor holding the evidence rather than the op itself. Another is that the model never needed fine weights among the other ops on difference contexts, since the answer there was difference, and so it never learned to weigh them there. This study does not separate them. A model trained without difference contexts would show how close any model of this size comes to the target null, and so how much of the shortfall is the edit; it is in the backlog.
