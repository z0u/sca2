# Shaped suppression, rather than projecting the whole axis out

Every intervention we have specified for M2 removes the anchor direction outright, which is the crudest edit available and the one most exposed to the drift ex-2.1.6 found: if the whole cube has a common component on the axis, taking the axis away moves everything. A suppression shaped to act on the red-selective part of the response — a function of the component rather than its deletion — might degrade red behavior while leaving the color cube intact, and it would still have side-effects boundable from the geometry beforehand, which is the property the method is for. Design question to settle before the first transformer intervention.

We already tried this in M1 (autoencoders): the "intervention lobes" work in `scratch-m1-code/docs/m2-control/ex-2.1-intervention-lobe.ipynb` and the paper appendix `references/sca1-paper/asec_intervention_lobes.tex` define a bounded falloff $h(\alpha)$ — zero below an alignment threshold $a$, ramping to max strength $b$ with shape controlled by power $p$ (plus a Bézier variant for the rotation-based "repulsion" intervention) — instead of the aggressive $h(\alpha)=\alpha$ used in the main M1 text. Worth pulling forward as a starting point rather than re-deriving.

**2026-08-30, housekeeping** — the carry-over half is settled: the [D2.2 design](/docs/m2/d2.2/design.md) puts M1's shaped suppression in the eval contract's operator library, beside axis projection and weight ablation. What it does not do is pick the shaped suppression for any experiment — *suppress red* runs plain axis projection, and the fallback and dose-label machinery downstream is written against the projection operator too.

That ordering reads as deliberate, and it changes what this item is waiting on. The worry here is ex-2.1.6's drift: if the whole cube has a common component on the axis, taking the axis away moves everything. *Suppress red* measures precisely that — observed non-red damage against a geometric prediction — on checkpoints already in the store, with no training cost. So the number that says whether a shaped falloff is needed arrives from the plan's first and cheapest experiment. Nothing needs to block on settling this beforehand; what this item asks for now is that the shaped suppression be in the operator library when that contract gets built, and that the suppress-red damage figure be what picks the operator for the interventions after it.

**2026-09-01, design review** — the machinery this item banks is now load-bearing: the design's *suppress operation* experiment grades its dose by intervention strength (scale the removed component, γ: 0 → 1) rather than by stimulus, because op-relevance on a four-op table occupies too few levels to grade on. A strength-scaled projection is the simplest form of this; the shaped suppression's alignment-gated falloff is the fuller one, and the suppress-red damage figure still picks between them.

**2026-09-02, ex-2.2.1 prereg** — the shaped suppression is in the operator library (`sca.intervention.shaped_suppression`, M1's $h(\alpha)$ with threshold $a$, ceiling $b$, shape $p$), and [ex-2.2.1](/docs/m2/ex-2.2.1/report.py) runs it as an arm beside the plain projection. The published clean map already shows the case this item worried about: the `+` and `=` embeddings of the anchored primary sit on the axis at α ≈ 0.3 and 0.4 on every line, red or not, so a plain projection rotates those states by 17–24° everywhere. The `shaped` arm's threshold (0.5) is set to leave them alone; ex-2.2.1's non-red damage figures, plain against shaped, are what picks the operator for the op experiments.

**2026-09-03, ex-2.2.1 results** — the `shaped` arm is scored: non-red damage at zero, red accuracy about 0.6 against 0.09 under the projection, so it is the selective operator and removes about half of red. The half comes from the geometry of $h(\alpha)$ after re-normalization: pure red at $\alpha = 0.9$ lands at 0.38, and the blocks bring it back to about 0.33 at the middle slices, half of it above the threshold. The report's operator figure now draws where each state lands as well as how far it turns. The repulsion form, which shapes the landing alignment instead, is split out to [its own item](./repulsion-sets-the-landing-alignment.md); which of the two the op experiments adopt is still this item's question.
