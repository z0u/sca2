# Science skill: ask what can satisfy a term where it acts

Three habits from the [lean review](/docs/m2/embedding-lean/report.py) that the `science` skill does not yet carry.

First, a sibling of the "a factor changes more than the thing it is named for" rule: before a run, for each site a regularizer acts on, list what could satisfy it there that is not the concept. The pull at the embedding slice could only be met by a token-level feature, which predicted the lean before it was measured.

Second, a re-analysis-first habit for a puzzling result: an edit restricted to one set of slices at a time, and a look at what lies along e₁ in the embedding and readout tables, both on stored checkpoints, before a new run. Spill-by-position and the lean review were both of this kind and cost no training.

Third, when an experiment restricts one term, check what else the restriction switches off before reading the result. `anchor_slices` turns the anti term off with the pull, and ex-2.2.22's slice result was read as "restricting the pull spills more" while the anti term had also gone from those slices.

Asked for by the reviewer on the lean review, who also wants a note on the process the review followed; the replies file in the project files has it.
