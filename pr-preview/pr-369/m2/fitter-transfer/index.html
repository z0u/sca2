The runs are those of post-hoc-compare: the eight controls of the anneal confirmationanneal confirmation Trained the controls at seeds 712 to 719 that the fitters are fitted on, sharing initialization, batches, and corpus with the anchored runs. at seeds 712 to 719, where every fitter is fitted and scored, and the eight τ_ℓ fixed runs at the same seeds for the anchor, at full dose, as published.

The fitters are difference in means, the ridge probe, LEACE, and INLP, with the optimized direction beside them as a ceiling. Each is restricted to the configurations that need no role labels and edit like the anchor. These are pooled fits: fitted on every position of a context, and editing every position at every slice (the embedding and each block output). The anchored edit acts at the same slices and positions. Within that scope, the grid still offers the target (binary labels, or graded by the posterior, where the fitter can use either), sequential refit (on or off), the replacement (zero or the mean), the rank of INLP, and the dose (a quarter, a half, three quarters, or all of the component).

What crosses from one run to another is a configuration, never a direction. Every direction is fitted on the run it edits, from that run's own fit set; the other runs only decide which settings to fit it with. The figure lays out where the fits and the edits happen.

The selection rule is that of post-hoc-compare, applied within the scope above. Write \(R\) for the eight runs. For each run \(r \in R\) in turn, the run left out:

The anchored edit needs no such choice. Training put the op on e₁ at every slice and kept the other ops off it. So its full edit at every slice removes 0.88 of the op and spills on no run. That supports the case for placing the op during training: the same recipe works on every run, with no choice of slice. A fitter with a per-run slice choice still removes about as much as the anchor here (post-hoc-compare). So the case is about carrying a recipe to a new model. And the probe on slices 1 to 3 shows that a fitter recipe can carry over part of the way.

Is the every-slice shape a fair thing to ask of a fitter? It is the shape the anchor was trained for, so it is not where a fitter is at its best. A practitioner using a steering direction or an eraser usually sweeps the layer and the strength on held-out data for each model, which is what the per-run choice of post-hoc-compare did, and there the fitters matched the anchor. But editing every layer is also a published procedure: concept scrubbing, in the paper that introduced LEACE (Belrose et al., 2023), fits LEACE at each layer in turn on the states as they arrive after the erasures before it, which is our every-slice scope with sequential refit. So E1 tests that procedure, carried to a new run, and E3 is closer to what a practitioner tuning one model would do.

When would carrying a recipe over matter, in place of tuning each model? When the same removal has to be applied to many models (checkpoints of one run, fine-tunes, a family of sizes); when the model can't be scored for spill before it is used, because the labelled data or the scoring isn't at hand; and when a model is retrained and the concept has to come out again. The placement also travels with the weights. A model trained with an anchor could say in its model card that difference is on e₁, where a fitted direction would have to be published beside the weights as an arbitrary vector, and refitted for each new model. With one model and a budget to tune on it, a fitter chosen per model does as well as the anchor here. Gradient routing (Cloud et al., 2024) makes the same case for its own training-time placement: a concept or a behaviour routed to known parameters during training can be removed or overseen later without searching for it. Its examples include removing a capability from a language model and overseeing a reinforcement learner by localizing the modules responsible for its behaviours.

This covers one concept, one small model, and eight runs per arm. It is also a re-analysis added after the comparison it follows, with E3 added after its own first results, so it describes what we saw. The doses come in quarters, so a choice between a quarter and a half is a mix of the two scores, which may differ a little from the score at a dose in between. A fitter with a dose per slice (smaller at the early slices) might do better at every slice; that would be a design of ours with no published baseline behind it, and the grid does not have it.
