Ex 2.2.20: a high-rate head start before the recipe schedule¶

We train the seven-op set for 200 epochs on a schedule of two cycles: a short one at a high learning rate, then the recipe schedule for the rest of the run, or the same with a lower second peak. Does that keep more of the skill of the 400-epoch recipe than a plain 200-epoch run? Neither schedule did, so we keep the plain one.

Findings¶

  • The head start keeps most of the skill (H1) — miss. The head-start runs fall short of the 400-epoch runs, inside the partial band, but all three HSV-channel ops fall short by more than their tolerance.
  • The head start beats the plain schedule (H2) — miss. The head start scores above the plain 200-epoch run at 1 of the three seeds, but below it on average. We keep the plain schedule.
  • The HSV-channel ops come sooner (H3) — miss. They came later than in the plain run at two out of three seeds.
  • Where the first cycle leaves off (E1) — the head start leads the plain run at epoch 50, and the second warmup takes the lead away within 5 epochs.
  • Calibration (E2) — the head start is about as well calibrated as the plain 200-epoch run.
  • Op confusion (E3) — the head start puts more mass off the diagonal in the HSV-channel rows than the plain 200-epoch run does.
  • A lower second peak (E4) — misses both rules by a wider margin.

How to read this report

This report was preregistered: the hypotheses, their gates, and the adoption rule were frozen at commit 2ea0b76, before any run of this experiment. Each section opens with what we expect, and the results replaced the placeholders in place.

Why¶

Ex-2.2.19 adopted 200 epochs for the seven-op set (no-four), half the length of the recipe, as a partial pass. On three fresh seeds the 200-epoch runs fell short of the 400-epoch runs by 0.0157 on average, against a gate of 0.015, and nearly all of the shortfall was in the three HSV-channel ops.

A short run at a high rate gets a long way quickly. The 50-epoch run of ex-2.2.19 at a peak learning rate of 0.00562 ended at a skill of 0.54, where the 200- and 400-epoch runs had reached between 0.32 and 0.39 by epoch 50. But it ends before the HSV-channel ops are learned, which in the 200-epoch runs happened between epochs 80 and 112.

So we try both in one run: 50 epochs on the schedule of that short run, then 150 epochs on the recipe schedule from where it leaves off. If the second cycle builds on the first, the run should end closer to the 400-epoch runs than a plain 200-epoch run does, at the same cost.

The runs¶

Each run follows the 200-epoch recipe of ex-2.2.19 with a different learning-rate schedule. The first cycle warms up over 5 epochs to 0.00562 and follows a cosine down to 1% of 0.00316 at epoch 50. The second warms up from there over 5 epochs to 0.00316, and follows a cosine down to 1% of that at epoch 200.

The second cycle is the recipe schedule of a 150-epoch run, step for step.1 The first cycle is the schedule of the ex-2.2.19 run at 0.00562, except that it ends where the second warmup starts. The optimizer state carries over between cycles, though resetting it should make no measurable difference.2

The schedule differs from the plain one in three ways at once: a higher peak for the first 50 epochs, a restart, and a final anneal 150 epochs long instead of 200. A result would say whether the schedule helps, and leave open which of the three did it.

A second head-start condition is the same, except that its second cycle peaks at 0.0021, about the highest rate at which a plain 200-epoch run of ex-2.2.19 learned the HSV-channel ops (H3). So it spends longer near those rates, and less time above them, than the first head start. From about epoch 80 on, its rate is close to that of the plain 200-epoch run, so the two differ mostly in the first 80 epochs.

Held-out expected exact match (EEM) is the probability that an answer drawn from the model at the query = is a correct answer of the true op. The Bayes ceiling is the same score for an ideal predictor. Skill is EEM rescaled so that a uniform guess scores 0 and the ceiling scores 1.

A run falls short of another by how much lower its EEM is, compared between runs that share a model seed and averaged over seeds. The hypotheses are scored on seeds 601–603, as H1 of ex-2.2.19 was. Seed 600 is shown beside them: the observation behind this experiment came from its runs, so it could flatter the head start.

The head start keeps most of the skill (H1)¶ ✗ Miss

What we expect. The head-start runs fall short of the 400-epoch runs by at most 0.015 on average, and no op falls short by more than its tolerance on average. That is a pass. A shortfall between 0.015 and 0.03 is a partial pass, and one beyond 0.03, or an op beyond its tolerance, is a miss. This is the H1 rule of ex-2.2.19, with the same tolerances.

What we saw. The head-start runs fell short of the 400-epoch runs by 0.0191 on average. That is inside the partial band, and a little more than the shortfall of the plain 200-epoch runs (0.0157). The shortfall is in the HSV-channel ops: each of the three falls short by more than its tolerance of 0.03, where none did in the plain runs.

Seed 600 scored 0.0019 above its 400-epoch run, so it did flatter the head start.

Miss

The head start falls short by 0.0191 on average, inside the partial band, but hue-hsv, sat-hsv, and value-hsv each fall short by more than their tolerance.

The head start beats the plain schedule (H2)¶ ✗ Miss

What we expect. The head-start run scores a higher EEM than the plain 200-epoch run at each of the three seeds, and so on average. That is a pass. A higher mean with a lower EEM at one or two seeds is a partial pass, and a mean gain at or below zero is a miss.

A miss would mean the second cycle kept nothing of the first, or lost it in the second warmup. H2 checks only the direction of the gain: the whole gap to the 400-epoch runs is small, so H1 covers its size.

Which schedule we adopt. H1 and H2 score the head start with the recipe peak, and E4 scores the one with a second peak of 0.0021 by the same rules. If both hypotheses pass for either schedule, the 200-epoch runs that follow will use that schedule. If both schedules pass, we take the one with the higher mean EEM over seeds 601–603. Otherwise we keep the plain schedule.

What we saw. The head start scored above the plain 200-epoch run at 1 of the three seeds, and 0.0034 below it on average. The ops that lose most are the HSV-channel ops again.

Miss

The head start scores below the plain 200-epoch run on average (-0.0034), above it at 1 of three seeds.

The HSV-channel ops come sooner (H3)¶ ✗ Miss

What we expect. In the plain 200-epoch runs, the mean skill of the HSV-channel ops first passed 0.5 between epochs 80 and 112. We expect the head-start runs to pass it about 25 epochs sooner, paired by seed (a gap estimated from the skill curves of ex-2.2.19).

The runs of ex-2.2.19 already suggest these ops do not wait for the learning rate to fall to some level. The 400-epoch runs passed 0.5 between epochs 80 and 112, close to the 200-epoch runs of the same seeds, but at rates between 0.0026 and 0.0029, higher than any rate at which a 200-epoch run passed it.

The 50- and 100-epoch runs fell through those rates to their floor, and their HSV-channel skill stayed at or below 0.35. That includes the 50-epoch run at 0.00562, the first cycle of the head-start schedule, which falls through all of those rates by about epoch 36.

So this schedule can separate elapsed time from learning progress. A rise at about the same epoch as in the plain run would say these ops wait for a number of epochs, whatever the model has learned by then. A sooner rise, as we expect, would say the progress of the first cycle carries over to them.

A later rise is possible too: the 200-epoch run of ex-2.2.19 at 0.00562 passed 0.5 only at epoch 144, so a high rate early may hold these ops back. The outcomes lie further apart than the 4-epoch logging interval. There is no gate, since no decision hangs on it.

What we saw. The HSV-channel ops came later in the head-start runs at two of the three seeds, by 28 and 56 epochs, and 20 epochs sooner at the third. The curves show the first cycle lifting the other ops well above the plain run by epoch 50, with the HSV-channel ops still near zero.

Miss

The HSV-channel ops came later at two of three seeds and sooner at one. The progress of the first cycle did not carry over to them.

Where the first cycle leaves off (E1)¶

Exploratory, with no prediction: how much of a head start the second cycle receives, and how much of it remains after the second warmup. We show the skill of the head-start runs at epoch 50, overall and per op, beside the plain runs at the same epoch and the 50-epoch ex-2.2.19 run at 0.00562 (seed 600 only).

The head start leads the plain run at epoch 50 over all ops, while the HSV-channel skill is still low in both. The second warmup takes the lead away by epoch 55. The head start with a second peak of 0.0021 keeps more of its lead, but ends lower all the same (E4).

Calibration (E2)¶

Exploratory, with no prediction. We show the calibration KL of each run beside its EEM: the KL divergence3 from the Bayes answer distribution to the answer distribution of the model. In ex-2.2.19 the 200- and 400-epoch runs were equally well calibrated, so a head start that buys EEM at some cost to calibration would show here.

The head-start runs have a calibration KL between 0.288 and 0.357, and the plain 200-epoch runs between 0.314 and 0.336, at seeds 601–603. The head start with a second peak of 0.0021 is between 0.305 and 0.380. Among the 200-epoch runs, KL falls as EEM rises, so the runs that score lower are also less well calibrated.

Op confusion (E3)¶

Exploratory, with no prediction. We show the op confusion matrix of each schedule, as in ex-2.2.19. For confident contexts of each true op, it gives the probability mass the model puts on the answers of each op, averaged over seeds 601–603.

In ex-2.2.19 the 200-epoch runs put more mass off the diagonal than the 400-epoch runs, mostly in the HSV-channel rows. This shows whether the head start moves that mass back.

The head start moves more of that mass off the diagonal, in the same rows. In the HSV-channel rows it puts 0.093 of the mass off the diagonal on average, and the head start with a second peak of 0.0021 puts 0.121, against 0.073 for the plain 200-epoch run and 0.042 at 400 epochs. In the other rows all four are between 0.031 and 0.040.

A lower second peak (E4)¶

Exploratory, with no prediction. We score the head start with a second peak of 0.0021 by the rules of H1 and H2. As in H3, we also show the epoch at which its HSV-channel skill first passes 0.5, beside the same epoch for the other head start.

Its results sit beside the other head start in the H1, H2, and H3 figures and tables. It misses both H1 and H2. It falls short of the 400-epoch runs by 0.0300 on average, at the edge of the partial band, with the three HSV-channel ops each beyond their tolerance. It scores below the plain 200-epoch run at all three seeds, by 0.0144 on average.

Its HSV-channel ops came 44 and 44 epochs later than in the plain run at two seeds, and 36 sooner at the third.

What it means for what follows¶

The 200-epoch runs that follow keep the plain schedule, as ex-2.2.19 adopted it.

The head start does what the 50-epoch run of ex-2.2.19 suggested, while it lasts: by the end of the first cycle the model has learned most ops outside the HSV channel. The second warmup then takes the lead away (E1). The second cycle seems to relearn those ops rather than build on the first.

The HSV-channel ops, which the first cycle had not learned, came no sooner (H3), and the head-start runs ended with more of their probability mass on the answers of the wrong op (E3).

The lower second peak was meant to give those ops more time at the rates where the plain runs learned them, yet it ended furthest short (E4). So time near those rates does not seem to be what the HSV-channel ops wait for. We hold this loosely: the schedule changes three things at once, and the three seeds disagree about the timing.

One question stays open: does the restart itself cost the lead? A schedule that hands over from the first cycle without a second warmup would answer it.

Method¶

Recipe. The unanchored d64-L4 model with an untied readout and the newline mask, as in ex-2.2.19, on the no-four corpus condition that ex-2.2.18 built (k3-r0.3). The runs differ from the plain 200-epoch runs of ex-2.2.19 in the learning-rate schedule alone.

Measurements. EEM, ceiling, floor, and calibration KL on the 2,000 held-out contexts per op, with the evaluation of ex-2.2.18. Skill curves are logged at about 50 points per run on 200 held-out contexts per op, every 4 epochs at 200 epochs, so an epoch of first passing is known to within that.

Cost. The new runs cost about $1.04 in all, scaled from the cost of an ex-2.2.18 run.

Per-op tolerances. As in ex-2.2.19: the seed range of the gap of each op over the three ex-2.2.17 runs, or 0.03, whichever is larger.

  1. The schedule is a dopesheet: keyframes at the start and peak of each warmup and at the end of each cosine, joined by straight lines going up and half cosines going down. At 264 steps an epoch, the second cycle matches the recipe schedule of a 150-epoch run to within 8e-07 of its peak. ↩

  2. Adam keeps running averages of the gradient and of its square. These decay with time constants of about 10 and 20 steps. The second warmup lasts 1,320 steps, so a reset at the end of the first cycle would be forgotten early in it, while the rate is still near its floor. ↩

  3. A measure of how far one probability distribution is from another; it is zero when they match. ↩
