The pool over slices 1 to 3 removed about as much and cost the task least, but its edit still spilled past the criterion on some seeds. Scope and method¶

This is an exploratory re-analysis, with no preregistration and no gate. It covers three conditions at model seeds 704 to 711, 400 epochs each, all with the falloff anti weight:

  • falloff, the recipe of record: the step pull on slices 2 to 4, pooled over positions at a fixed temperature \(\tau_p\) of 0.1 (the runs of split-anti-seeds).
  • step, soft τ: the same step, with \(\tau_p\) starting at 1 and sharpening to 0.1 by the end of the pull warm-up at epoch 40 (soft-pool).
  • pool 1–3, soft τ: one pull pooled over the slice and position pairs of slices 1 to 3, with both temperatures starting soft (\(\tau_p\) 1 → 0.1, and the slice temperature \(\tau_\ell\) 4 → 0.4) and nothing pulling slice 4 (soft-pool).

The runs at a seed share their initialization, batches, and label draws, and the control of ex-2.2.23 at that seed (trained without the anchor) shares them too. So each comparison is paired by seed. A difference between two conditions is set against the spread of the paired differences over the eight seeds, rather than against the seed range of either condition. We report the mean paired difference from falloff, with its range and the count of seeds on each side of zero.

The comparison is of recipes, not of factors. pool 1–3, soft τ differs from step, soft τ in which slices it pulls as well as in pooling over them. So a gap between those two cannot be put down to either change alone. Soft-pool lists what each change moves.

Eight seeds are too few to compare how often a recipe loses the anchor. None of the 24 runs here lost it. The loss-rate screen counted losses at 40 seeds each for falloff (one loss) and step, soft τ (none), and bounded the rates at about one in nine and one in fourteen. The pool has no count beyond these eight. This report quotes those counts and does not add to them.

The loss-rate screen trained seven of its runs to 400 epochs: the four falloff runs it did not count as kept (one of them the lost run), and three step, soft τ runs it finished as a check. They have no control at their seeds, so removalRemoval How much of difference the full edit takes out: the drop in its score, as a share of the way from the clean score to the target null, net of the median baseline. Higher is better. Removal with nothing subtracted is shown beside it. and spillSpill The side effect of the edit on the other ops: the largest drop in the score of any other op at any dose, net of the median baseline. A run is within the selectivity criterion when it is at most 0.02. are scored against the median baselineMedian baseline What an arbitrary direction costs: the median drop when the same edit is made along each of the other 63 coordinate axes of the same run, per op and dose (the rule of the D2.2 design). alone. They were picked as slow or doubtful seeds, so they are a check on the slow end and not a sample of either condition.

Their task changeTask change What the anchor costs the task: the clean score per op, less that of the control at the same seed. is taken against the mean of the eight controls at 704 to 711, as loss-rate took it. The eight controls differ from one another by less than a fiftieth on the mean score, which is about how far the choice of control could move it.

Every edit is scored on the 14,000 held-out contexts of ex-2.2.21, 2,000 per op. The edit takes a share of the e₁ component out of the state at every position and every slice. That share is the dose: a quarter, a half, three quarters, or all of it. The measurements are defined in the glossary at the end.

What the edit does (E2)¶

The measurements of E1 are means over all the contexts of an op. Edit-effects showed on falloff that the edit also behaves well context by context. The blocks downstream put back less e₁ on the other ops than the clean model has. The drop on each difference context follows how much that context needed the op. Context by context, the three behave alike. Past the embedding the write stays close to the bound under every recipe, and from slice 2 on the other ops arrive with much less e₁ than the clean model gives them, the same in all three. The pool is where they differ, at the first two slices. There the other ops sit closer to e₁ under the pool: the bound at their example answers is about 3× as wide at the embedding as under falloff, and about a third wider at slice 1.

At slice 1 under the pool, the write goes a little over the bound at the example operands, where the bound is a few degrees. That is the largest ratio of any recipe, and the only one past the tolerance of ex-2.2.1 at the seed mean, but in degrees it is small. More single sites go over the tolerance under the pool than under the step, all where the bound is 6° or less.

So the pool does not change what the edit does downstream. It changes how close the other ops sit to e₁ at the slices before the model has read the context. The step deliberately puts no pull there.

Where the evidence sits (E3)¶

The step puts the pull on slices 2 to 4, while the pool lets the pull choose among slices 1 to 3. In slice-terms, a pull pooled over blocks 1 to 4 at a fixed temperature settled on block 4 on most seeds and on the query answer on one, where the op could not be removed. Soft-pool found the pool over slices 1 to 3 peaking at slice 3 on average. Does it settle on the same slice at every seed, and does the edit find it there?

What the pull lands on is the evidence for the op. In example-evidence, αAlignment How far a state points along e₁: the cosine between the state and e₁, which for a unit-length state is its component along e₁; 1 on the anchor direction and 0 off it. at an example answer followed how well that one example fits difference, and in equals-evidence the anchor was nearly absent at the = sites, where the model infers the op itself. So this section follows where the evidence sits. Under each recipe the evidence settles in one place on every seed. Under the step, α at the example answers climbs to slice 4 and stays there, and the soft start changes nothing in that shape. The pool climbs higher at slice 1, peaks at slice 3 on every seed, and falls back at slice 4, where nothing pulls it. Its eight seeds lie nearly on top of one another, and none of them settled on the query answer.

The edit finds the op wherever its evidence settles. The edit at blocks 2 to 4 alone removes most of it under every recipe, except at seed 710. There it falls short under all three, as it did in every arm of split-anti-seeds, and the edit at block 1 alone removes nearly all of it. The edit at block 4 alone removes nothing in any recipe, the pool included. Under the pool the edit at block 1 alone removes more, and more evenly across seeds, than under the step, which fits the pool pulling there.

The embedding table also sets the pool apart, but it does not account for the spill on its own. Under the pool, the e₁ components of the color embeddings follow lightness more closely than under the step: darker colors sit further along e₁. The pool shows this on average and closely on every seed, while under the step it varies from seed to seed. In the control the same correlation scatters around zero.

That is the lean of embedding-lean, the stand-in the model offers for difference before it has read the context. It is also the reason the step leaves block 1 out of the pull. So the pool, by pulling slice 1, seems to bring back part of the lean.

But the edit at the embedding alone, which takes that lean out, spills nothing. Discussion¶

On these eight seeds step, soft τ is about as good a recipe as falloff. It removes a couple of hundredths less, spillsSpill The side effect of the edit on the other ops: the largest drop in the score of any other op at any dose, net of the median baseline. A run is within the selectivity criterion when it is at most 0.02. no more, and costs the task about as little. The edit also acts the same way context by context. Its one run below the task gateTask gate The task criterion of the D2.2 design: it fails if the task change falls below −0.02 on the mean over ops, or below −0.05 on any one op. is a loss-rate run with no control at its seed. And falloff has a run of its own past the per-op line, so on the task the two look about level.

The measure that could still set them apart is how often each loses the anchor, which these runs cannot add to. Loss-rate found no losses in 40 for the soft start and one for falloff, a difference too small at those counts to rest on.

pool 1–3, soft τ is close behind on removalRemoval How much of difference the full edit takes out: the drop in its score, as a share of the way from the clean score to the target null, net of the median baseline. Higher is better. Removal with nothing subtracted is shown beside it. and ahead on the task, and the evidence settles in the same place on every seed, which is what one would want of a recipe that chooses the slice for itself. Its shortfall is spill.

The pool still has a range, slices 1 to 3, but the range follows from the architecture rather than from what we know of the latent representations. The embedding holds no context, so the inferred op cannot exist there. And at slice 4 no block is left to carry what sits at an example answer to the query =, so the op there cannot reach the prediction. Slices 1 to 3 are the only ones where it can do both, which carries to another model by its depth alone.

The edit at block 1 is where the spill enters, and block 1 is the one slice the pool pulls and the step leaves alone. But E2 shows the other ops sitting closer to e₁ at the first two slices under the pool. How hard and how early the pull acts at slice 1 is set by the schedules of the anchor weight \(\lambda_a\) and the slice temperature \(\tau_\ell\), and this pool has not been tuned on either.
