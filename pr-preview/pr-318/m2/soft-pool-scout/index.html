Soft pool scout¶

A sharper pool over slices stopped the early spillSpill The side effect of an edit on the other ops: the largest drop in the score of any op other than the one removed, net of what the same edit does to a control trained without the anchor. The selectivity criterion of D2.2 allows at most 0.02 at any dose. of pool 1–3, soft τ. Of sixteen schedules of the pooled pull screened at epoch 64, those with the slice temperature ending at 0.1 had no run spill early, however soon or late the temperatures sharpened.

We carried two of these conditions on to epoch 400. Both stay within the spill criterion on every seed, and both remove a little more than the recipe of record at no cost to the task. Holding the slice temperature at 0.1 from the start is simpler and cleaner, and a candidate for the recipe of record.

Recipe-compare left one thing between pool 1–3, soft τ and the recipe of record: two of its eight runs go over the spill criterion, and the spill enters at block 1, the one slice the pool pulls and the step leaves alone. Spill-timing found that both of those runs spill early, while the pool sharpens, and that this early kind already shows at epoch 64. So a schedule that keeps block 1 closed should show it at epoch 64, at a sixth of the cost of a full run.

Stage 1 trains sixteen conditions of the pool at its eight seeds (704 to 711), each changing when the two pool temperatures sharpen, how sharp the pool over slices ends, or when the anchor weight warms up. Every run stops at epoch 64, where we score whether it is keeping the anchor and how much the edit spills. In stage 2, the two conditions with \(\tau_\ell\) ending at 0.1 that change least from the base (sharpening by epoch 40 as the base does, and held at 0.1 from the start) are carried on to epoch 400 from where they stopped, and measured as recipe-compare measured the three recipes, with the spill along training from checkpoints kept on the way.

The other ops sit as far from e₁ at the first two slices as under the step, and the lean of the color embeddings along lightness falls back to the level of the step.

Scope and method¶

This is an exploratory study, with no preregistration and no gate. Every condition starts from pool 1–3, soft τ (the base, which is not the recipe of record): the pull is pooled over the slice and position pairs of slices 1 to 3, the position pool temperature \(\tau_p\) sharpens from 1 to 0.1 and the slice pool temperature \(\tau_\ell\) from 4 to 0.4 over the first forty epochs, and the anchor weight \(\lambda_a\) warms up over the same forty epochs to its peak of 0.1. The anti weight is that of falloff, the recipe of record, and the rest of training is as in soft-pool.

Twelve conditions cross two factors. One is \(\tau_\ell\) at the end, with levels 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4 (the base), each starting from 4. The other is when the temperatures are sharp: by epoch 10, 20 or 40 (the base), both on the same window, or \(\tau_\ell\) at its end value from epoch 0 while \(\tau_p\) still sharpens by 40, which tells the two temperatures apart. Five more conditions move the \(\lambda_a\) warm-up at the base temperatures: a delay (zero until epoch 20, then up by 60) and a stretch (up by 80), each with the soft temperatures and with both temperatures fixed at their end values from the start, and the fixed temperatures with the base warm-up. The fixed-temperature conditions might have lost the anchor, which is what they were there to show: if a delayed pull keeps it at a fixed τ, the soft start was a delay by other means. The schedules are drawn below.

Each run trains on the full 400-epoch schedule and stops at epoch 64, keeping what it needs to carry on (a resumed run matches a straight run bit for bit). The base is not trained again: spill-timing trained it at these seeds with the same scoring at every record, so its epoch-64 record is the base here. The epoch-64 checkpoints of falloff and step, soft τ left by the loss-rate screen are reference points on the same scale.

A screen at epoch 64 can rank the conditions by the early kind of spillSpill The side effect of an edit on the other ops: the largest drop in the score of any op other than the one removed, net of what the same edit does to a control trained without the anchor. The selectivity criterion of D2.2 allows at most 0.02 at any dose. and by lost anchors. It cannot clear a condition: the late kind of spill, which grows on value-hsv after epoch 100 on seeds that learn it late, is invisible here. Spill-timing found the base about 0.02 above the quiet recipes at epoch 64, pooled over seeds, so the seed mean of eight runs can tell an early-spilling condition from a quiet one at about that resolution, and a single run says little. We compare many conditions, so a mean a little apart from its neighbors is a lead only. The pattern across a factor, which pools 32 runs at each level, is firmer.

Stage 2¶

Each run of the two finished conditions (\(\tau_\ell\) ending at 0.1 and sharpening by epoch 40, and \(\tau_\ell\) held at 0.1 from the start while \(\tau_p\) sharpens by 40) carries on from epoch 64 to epoch 400. The checkpoints kept at epochs 16, 32, 64, 128, and 256 along the way are scored for the spill.

We measure the finished runs as recipe-compare measured falloff, step, soft τ, and the base at the same seeds, and set them beside its numbers for those three. Each run at a seed shares its initialization, batches, and label draws with the base and with the control of ex-2.2.23 at that seed. So each comparison is paired by seed, and the paired difference from the base says whether a gap is larger than the variation between seeds.

  • Removal: the drop in the score of difference under the full edit, as a share of the way from the clean score to the target nullTarget null Where a removal should land: what an ideal predictor that had lost one op and nothing else would answer. It is the Bayes posterior over ops given the examples, with the removed op set to zero and the rest renormalized, so it still weighs the other ops by how well they fit.. It is net of the median drop under the same edit along each of the other 63 coordinate axes of the run (the rule of the D2.2 design). A run that removes less than 0.5 has lost the anchor.
  • Spill: the largest drop on another op at any dose, net of the same median. The criterion is 0.02.
  • Task change: the clean score per op, less that of the control at the seed. The task gate fails below -0.02 on the mean over ops, or below -0.05 on any one op.
  • Spill along training: the spill at each kept checkpoint and at the end, against eight reference edits, as at the stop.
  • Restricted edits, the write, the grading, and the landing, as in recipe-compare: the full edit made at a set of slices only, net of the same median as removal and spill; how much e₁ the blocks after the edit put back on the other ops, against the bound the clean pass sets; the share removed on difference contexts; and how far the edited answers move toward the target null.

Eight seeds are too few to add to a count of lost anchors. The loss-rate screen took that count at forty seeds for the step recipes, and nobody has taken it for the pool.

Lost anchors (E1)¶

The fixed-temperature conditions were the ones most likely to lose the anchor, since a sharp pool from the first step is what let the pull latch onto the query answer on one seed in slice-terms. Did any schedule lose it by epoch 64?

None did. Every run is called kept, and the lowest call (0.36, on a stretched warm-up) is still above the line by a wide margin. The figure (top) shows the calls close together across all sixteen conditions. The fixed temperatures and the stretch sit a little lower than the rest, which fits a pull that has had less time at full strength by epoch 64, but none comes near the uncertain band.

Two soft starts are in play: \(\tau_p\) sharpening from 1, and \(\tau_\ell\) sharpening from 4. The conditions with \(\tau_\ell\) fixed drop the second, the fixed-τ conditions drop both, and all of them kept the anchor on every seed. So in this pool neither soft start is what keeps the anchor, at least to epoch 64 and at these seeds. The step may differ: in the loss-rate screen, the step at a fixed \(\tau_p\) (falloff) lost the anchor on one seed of forty and the step with the soft start on none, which fits a soft \(\tau_p\) that helps the step, though one loss is too few to show it. Eight seeds would miss a loss rate of one in ten or so, and a run can still lose the anchor late, so the fixed temperatures are not cleared for this pool either.

Spill at epoch 64 (E2)¶

If spillSpill The side effect of an edit on the other ops: the largest drop in the score of any op other than the one removed, net of what the same edit does to a control trained without the anchor. The selectivity criterion of D2.2 allows at most 0.02 at any dose. enters through block 1 while the pool is soft, then sharpening sooner, sharpening the slice pool further, or holding the pull back until e₁ is cleared might each close it. Which of them lowers the spill at epoch 64?

The slice temperature at the end does, and the others barely move it. The figure (bottom) groups the crossing by \(\tau_\ell\): at 0.1 the four timing levels all sit at or below the quiet recipes, with no run of 32 over the criterion; at 0.2 the early spill is a little higher, with 3 runs over; at 0.4, the base level, 9 of 32 are over. Within each level, sharpening by epoch 10, 20 or 40, or holding \(\tau_\ell\) fixed from the start, makes no difference we can see at eight seeds. So for the early spill, what matters is how sharp the slice pool ends, and how soon it gets there barely does. That also covers where the slice pool starts: at 0.1, sharpening from 4 and holding at 0.1 from epoch 0 spill about the same, so a start in between would be unlikely to differ.

This is a stronger effect of \(\tau_\ell\) than slice-terms saw. Its E4 trained the pool over slices with \(\tau_\ell\) from 0.1 to 1.6 and found no trend in removal, with the spill a little higher at the two softest levels. But that was one seed, chosen because its pull had settled on block 4, at a fixed \(\tau_p\) and with the pool over blocks 1 to 4. Here the pool leaves block 4 out, and the spill is a per-seed event: at \(\tau_\ell\) 0.4 most seeds stay quiet, and one seed would most likely have been one of those.

The \(\lambda_a\) conditions all stay well above the \(\tau_\ell\) 0.1 level. One of them is not on an equal footing: under the stretched warm-up, \(\lambda_a\) is still at 94% of its peak at the stop, so its spill at epoch 64 is of a pull not yet at full strength. A twenty-epoch delay, which gives e₁ time to clear under the full learning rate, lowered the mean spill somewhat, at the soft temperatures and at the fixed ones. The stretched warm-up raised it, and so did fixing both temperatures with the base warm-up. Each of these means rests on one or two seeds that spill far more than the rest, so at eight seeds the differences among them could be chance. None of them comes near what sharpening the slice pool does.

Of the 21 runs over the criterion, 15 spill most on darken, the op on which the base spills early in spill-timing.

These are early numbers. A sharper slice pool could still spill late, on value-hsv as the base does on its slow seeds, and only a finished run can show that. What we take from the screen is that \(\tau_\ell\) is the lever on the early kind, and that the level at 0.1 brings the pool to the quiet recipes on it.

A sharper slice pool takes more of the pull off slice 1 and puts it on slice 3, so block 1 holds less of the anchor and the edit has less to take. It fits without proving it; the screen shows the two moving together, and a restricted edit on the finished runs (block 1 alone) would test it.

Spill along training (E5)¶

Spill-timing found two kinds of spillSpill The side effect of an edit on the other ops: the largest drop in the score of any op other than the one removed, net of what the same edit does to a control trained without the anchor. Which kind is left on the seeds that come near the criterion at epoch 400?

The late kind. Under the base, three seeds go over the criterion by epoch 64, and 711 does so already at epoch 32. Under the two finished conditions no seed goes over it by then, and up to epoch 128 the largest spill is 0.015 under τ_ℓ 0.1, by 40 and 0.003 under τ_ℓ 0.1, τ_ℓ fixed. After that, two seeds under τ_ℓ 0.1, by 40 (705 and 707) and one under τ_ℓ 0.1, τ_ℓ fixed (707) climb to about the criterion by the end. So the screen at epoch 64 called the early kind correctly: the conditions it found quiet stayed quiet through the part of training it covers.

The late kind here is about as large as on the late seeds of the base (705 and 710 come to about 0.02 there too). It lands on different ops from those spill-timing saw, though: on hue-hsv and darken at 705 and 707 under τ_ℓ 0.1, by 40, and on mix at 707 under τ_ℓ 0.1, τ_ℓ fixed, where the late kind in the base was on value-hsv. Two or three runs are too few to say whether that is a different kind of late spill or the same one landing elsewhere.

So are most of the signs that block 1 is open to the edit. The wider bound at the first two slices and the lean in the embedding table are gone under both conditions, and the larger cost of the block-1 edit is gone under the condition with \(\tau_\ell\) fixed.

Of the two, holding \(\tau_\ell\) at 0.1 from the start is the cleaner. It removes the most and the most evenly, has no run over the criterion under either median, and one under the edit at block 1 alone, as each step recipe has. It also has one schedule fewer than the base, since only \(\tau_p\) sharpens.

The other condition is close behind on the full edit, where the gap rests on two or three runs near the line. On the edit at block 1 alone the gap is larger: there the other condition sits near the base on most seeds, and only its mean is lower.

What is left is the late kind of spill, which brings one or two seeds per condition to about the criterion by the end. It is about as large as under the base on its late seeds, so it does not seem to come from the sharper slice pool, and nothing in this study acts on it. When it appears, the term still changing is the anti weight, partway through its anneal, so if anything in the recipe acts on the late kind, that anneal is the likelier place.

Before the pool with \(\tau_\ell\) fixed at 0.1 could replace falloff as the recipe of record, it would need two things the step has: a count of lost anchors at forty seeds, which a loss-rate screen would give, and a set of fresh seeds, which a confirmation at seeds 712 to 719 with their controls would give. Since the pool chooses the slice within a range set by the architecture, it would also be the easier recipe to carry to another model, as recipe-compare argued.
