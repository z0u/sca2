Fixed labels¶

Every anchored run so far drew its labels afresh on each visit to a context, so over a run the anchor saw labels on most difference contexts. Holding one draw fixed for the run, as a real labelled set would be, still keeps the anchor at every rate we tried. But the edit removes the op less reliably: a few runs end well below the rest, and more labels in the fixed set did not cure it.

Writing it up, we noticed that the anchor labeller (inherited from M1) makes a fresh 1/50 draw on every visit to a context.

An epoch covers about a quarter of the training split, so over 400 epochs a context is in view about a hundred times. With a fresh draw on each, about four fifths of the 42,891 difference contexts carry a label at some point (about 34,000), far more distinct labels than "one context in fifty" suggests. A real pipeline labels a fixed set once.

This report trains the recipe of record, τ_ℓ fixed, with one draw made per run and held for the whole run. We try three rates: 1/50 (about 870 labelled difference contexts), 1/25 (about 1,740), and 1/10 (about 4,320). Scope and method¶

This is an exploratory study, with no preregistration and no gate of its own. It started as a scout: one seed at 1/50, trained to epoch 64. Then 1/50 and 1/10 ran at four seeds to the end of training. At four seeds 1/50 removed less than 1/10, so they were carried to eight seeds, and 1/25 was added between them. The three rates were each chosen before their runs, and none was chosen by how it scored.

Every run is τ_ℓ fixed as the fixed confirmationfixed confirmation τ_ℓ fixed, with the per-visit label draw, keeps the anchor on all eight runs at seeds 712 to 719 with removal near 0.9, and is the recipe of record for D2.2. trained it, with one change to the labels. In place of the per-visit draw, a single draw made before training picks the labelled contexts. A picked context carries the label on every visit, and any other context never does.

Each seed has its own random stream for this draw, and the draws are nested, so every context labelled at 1/50 is also labelled at 1/25 and at 1/10. The per-visit draw is still taken and set aside, so each run trains on the same batches from the same initialization as the per-visit run at its seed. Only which contexts carry the pull differs.

The pull is a mean over the labelled contexts in each batch, so the label rate does not change how hard the anchor pulls. At 1/50 it also leaves the number of labelled contexts per batch unchanged on average. What the rate changes is how many distinct contexts the pull ever falls on, and how much the pull in a batch varies from one batch to the next.

We compare conditions as two samples of eight runs (the D2.2 rule since the determinism decision), with a 95% Welch t interval on the difference of seed means.11 Welch t interval: a confidence interval for a difference of two means that does not assume the two samples have equal variance. Every run is called kept, at every rate, with a wide margin. The rise over the first 40 epochs looks much the same in all four conditions. Over the call window, the seed mean of the fixed 1/50 runs is a little below that of the per-visit runs. The 1/25 and 1/10 runs are a little above it, and closer together. All of them are near the spread of the per-visit runs.

So a fixed set of under a thousand labelled contexts is enough for the anchor to settle. But across the fixed-label runs, mean α over the call window says little about how much a run removes at the end (Pearson r = 0.04 over the 24 runs).

Removal does not climb with the label count. At 1/25 every run is within the per-visit range, and the seed means match.

At 1/50 and at 1/10 most runs remove about as much as the per-visit runs, but a few end below 0.8, under the lowest per-visit run (0.83): two at 1/50 (0.60 to 0.66) and three at 1/10 (0.66 to 0.75). Those runs pull the seed mean down by about the same amount at both rates.

The two rates have their low runs mostly at different seeds, and share only seed 719. At those seeds, the per-visit runs range from the lowest per-visit run (seed 715) to the second highest (seed 717; the grey lines in the figure). So the low runs look more like single runs that happened to end low than like seeds that are hard for the recipe.

All three low runs at 1/10 are at seeds 717 to 719, which is why the first look at four seeds missed them.

When the low runs fall behind (E3)¶

Do the low runs fail to settle, or settle and then lose ground? The answer says whether the shortfall is about the anchor finding e₁ or about holding it there.

Both happen. Some low runs are already behind at epoch 32 and never catch up, staying near 0.6 for the rest of training. Others come close to the per-visit level by epoch 100 or so and then slide down over the rest of the run, while the anti weight anneals. Under the per-visit draw, slow starters (such as seed 717, still near zero at epoch 32) catch up by the end.

Neither kind lines up with the rate. Both appear at 1/50 and at 1/10, and the 1/25 runs show neither. The 1/25 runs also reach their final level sooner than any other condition, by about epoch 64. With eight runs a condition, that may be the same luck of the seeds as in E2.

One account fits, though we have not tested it. With a fixed set, the pull keeps returning to the same few hundred or thousand contexts, so what the anchor learns depends on which contexts the draw happened to pick. A draw whose contexts cover the examples or the replacement noise unevenly could anchor the op only partly, and keep it that way.

The per-visit draw averages over almost every context, which would explain why its runs spread less. Telling this apart from plain run-to-run sensitivity would take several draws at the same seed.

Discussion¶

A fixed labelled set at one context in fifty is enough for the anchor to settle on e₁ and keep it (E1, E2). That was the main worry, since the per-visit draw had shown the anchor most of the difference contexts over a run. On that count, the scarce-label claim of the D2.2 design holds with labels as a real pipeline would have them.

But removalRemoval The drop on difference under the full edit at every position and slice, as a share of the way from the clean score to the target null. Since a fixed set five times larger did no better, more labels of the same kind do not seem to be the fix, at least between about 900 and 4,300 contexts. The per-visit draw spreads less, and that may come from the variety of contexts it sees rather than their number.

With that spread, eight runs give a noisy seed mean.
