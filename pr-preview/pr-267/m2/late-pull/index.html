The embedding-lean experiment found that every anchored run of ex-2.2.23 leans lightness onto e₁ in its color embedding table. At the embedding there is no context yet for the pull to use, so the pull settles for a token-level stand-in: darker colors are the nearest one for difference answers. Editing only blocks 2 to 4 of those runs removed most of difference with almost no spill. Editing the embedding alone spilled as much as editing every slice.

Pulling every slice but the embedding had been tried before (ex-2.2.21 and the τ × λₐ sweep). But those runs also left the anti-subspace termAnti-subspace term The companion to the pull: the mean of α² over every position and slice it acts on, which asks the states as a whole to stay off e₁. off the embedding, and the table leaned all the same.

So this experiment trains twelve runs at 400 epochs and 4 seeds:

  • deep: the pull on blocks 2 to 4 only, and the anti-subspace term on every slice, so the first two slices are asked to stay off e₁ rather than left alone.
  • deep-clean: the same, and after every step the e₁ component of every embedding is set to zero, so the embeddings cannot lean at all. The readout table is untied on this recipe (a separate matrix from the embedding table) and stays free.
  • whole: the recipe of record, every slice pulled, trained again to record the alignmentAlignment How far a state points along e₁: the cosine between the state and e₁, which is 1 on the anchor direction and 0 off it. at every slice and position along training.

Each run shares its seed, and so its initialization, batches and label draws, with one anchored run and one control of ex-2.2.23. The anchor weight, the pool temperature τ and both schedules are the recipe values. A second round of sixteen runs repeats deep and deep-clean with the anti-subspace weight annealed to a higher hold (E4).

Scope¶

This is an exploratory study, with no preregistration and no gate. Four seeds per arm can only resolve a large change in the leanLean Lightness on e₁ in the color embedding table: darker colors sit further along the anchor direction, a stand-in for difference at the slices where nothing contextual exists. or the spillSpill The largest net drop in score on another op under the edit. The selectivity criterion is a spill of at most 0.02., against the seed range. The whole arm reproduces its twins in ex-2.2.23 (the task scores of each pair agree to within 0.0001), so a difference between arms at one seed comes from the change of pull, not from nondeterminism in training.

Method¶

The anchor weight is the recipe value, and outside E4 the anti-subspace weight keeps its recipe schedule. That schedule is a multiple of the anchor weight: about 2.5× early in training, annealing to about 0.3× by about the midpoint.

The anchor term is a mean over the pulled slices, so at the same weight each of blocks 2 to 4 is pulled about 5/3 as hard in the new arms as in whole. The anti term is a mean over the same five slices in every arm, so it is unchanged.

The measurements are those of the embedding-lean experiment, taken on every run:

  • The lightness slope of the color embedding table: how much further along e₁ the color embeddings sit for black than for white, as the slope of the e₁ component against lightness over the 216 colors. It is zero in deep-clean, where the table is held off e₁.
  • The lightness correlation in the states: the correlation of αAlignment How far a state points along e₁: the cosine between the state and e₁, which is 1 on the anchor direction and 0 off it. with lightness at each slice, over the color positions of contexts of other ops.
  • RemovalRemoval How much of difference the edit takes out: the net drop in its score under the edit, as a share of the way from the clean score to the target null. and spillSpill The largest net drop in score on another op under the edit. The selectivity criterion is a spill of at most 0.02. of the edit, which projects e₁ out of the state at every position on a set of slices. Both are net of the control at the same seed, and the selectivity criterion is a spill of at most 0.02.
  • The task score of each op, as expected exact matchExpected exact match The task score, as the chance that a drawn answer agrees with a true one. The lightness in the states at block 1 fades too, to about half the recipe value, and in deep-clean it is smaller again. By block 2 it is small in every arm.

On the recipe the slope grows from early in training. The growth also coincides with the anneal of the anti-subspace weight, which E4 changes. Part of the lean may also come from the per-slice pull being 5/3 of the recipe value.

Removal and spill (E2)¶

The edit on every slice is the goal, since one that has to know which slices to skip is a weaker guarantee. The main measure is its spillSpill The largest net drop in score on another op under the edit. The spill falls, and removal holds. Under the edit on every slice all twelve runs remove about as much as each other. The recipe runs spill well above the criterion on every seed, and deep spills less on every seed but stays above it. deep-clean is within it on 3 of 4 runs, and the fourth spills about twice the criterion (0.038).

In both new arms the concept sits at block 1 as well as later. Editing block 1 alone removes at least as much as editing every slice, though block 1 is never pulled, and it gives most of the spill that is left. In deep the embedding alone still spills on seeds 700 and 702, where the weaker leanLean Lightness on e₁ in the color embedding table: darker colors sit further along the anchor direction, a stand-in for difference at the slices where nothing contextual exists. of E1 is still used downstream.

Editing only blocks 2 to 4 spills almost nothing in any arm, but its removal splits by seed. On seven of the twelve runs it removes nearly everything, and on the other five almost nothing (seeds 700 and 702 in deep, seeds 700 and 701 in deep-clean, and seed 700 in whole).

On those five, the answer depends on the e₁ component at block 1, and not on the e₁ component the pull put at blocks 2 to 4. Perhaps the later blocks read block 1 along e₁ and write the answer elsewhere. Or the two edits may differ in some other way that this measurement cannot tell apart. The embedding-lean experiment saw the same split on the recipe and took it as a sign that the concept was partly built from the lean. Here the split persists with the table held off e₁, so it does not need the lean.

Where the alignment settles (E3)¶

A concept that settles where the pull is should show up at blocks 2 to 4 on difference contexts and nowhere on the others. On the recipe every slice rises together on difference contexts, and the embedding and block 1 rise nearly as much on the other ops. That is the leanLean Lightness on e₁ in the color embedding table: darker colors sit further along the anchor direction, a stand-in for difference at the slices where nothing contextual exists. seen from the states: at those slices the alignment follows the tokens, whatever the op.

On both new arms, blocks 2 to 4 rise on difference contexts within the first few dozen epochs and then level off, and stay low on the other ops. Block 1 rises later and more slowly, on the other ops as well as on difference, though less on the others than on the recipe. So block 1 comes to meet part of the pull on block 2, and the anti term does not stop it.

Much of the rise at block 1, and nearly all of it on the other ops, comes after epoch 150, as the anti weight anneals (E4). Its rise on other ops is the spillSpill The largest net drop in score on another op under the edit. The selectivity criterion is a spill of at most 0.02. that E2 traced to the edit on block 1.

On deep the embedding also drifts onto e₁ in the second half of training, by about as much on the other ops as on difference. That is the whole color embedding table shifting a little toward e₁, with the lightness slope of E1 on top. It does not happen in deep-clean, where the table is held at zero.

The anti-subspace hold (E4)¶

In E1 and E3 the table and block 1 moved onto e₁ mostly while the anti-subspace weight was annealing. If the anneal lets go too early, holding that weight higher should keep them off.

A second round repeats deep and deep-clean with the anti weight annealed to a hold of 0.1 (equal to the anchor weight) or 0.2, in place of the recipe hold of 0.03. The peak at the start, the anneal at the end of training, and the seeds are unchanged.

At 0.2 the slope in deep is between −0.12 and 0.01 by seed. That is within the range of the controls, and no longer all one sign. Block 1 stays near its early level through training. At 0.1 both grow, but about half as much as at the recipe hold.

In deep-clean the hold changes the spill less, since it was already near the criterion, but removal falls about as much as in deep. At 0.2 one run (seed 703) spills 0.063, more than any deep-clean run at the recipe hold, and another (seed 701) removes only 0.49.

Discussion¶

Moving the pull off the first two slices did most of what the embedding-lean experiment expected of it. The color embedding table leansLean Lightness on e₁ in the color embedding table: darker colors sit further along the anchor direction, a stand-in for difference at the slices where nothing contextual exists. much less, the lightness in the states fades, and the full edit spillsSpill The largest net drop in score on another op under the edit. The selectivity criterion is a spill of at most 0.02. less on every seed. Holding the table off e₁ outright took the spill most of the rest of the way, with nothing lost in removalRemoval How much of difference the edit takes out: the net drop in its score under the edit, as a share of the way from the clean score to the target null. or in the task. That fits the account that the spill comes from stand-ins at the slices where nothing contextual exists.

It did not keep the concept out of block 1. The pull on block 2 is cheapest to meet by having block 1 already lean toward e₁, and block 1 has some context to work with, so part of what it puts there is about difference and part is shared with other ops.

Keeping out the shared part is the job of the anti-subspace termAnti-subspace term The companion to the pull: the mean of α² over every position and slice it acts on, which asks the states as a whole to stay off e₁., and on its recipe schedule it does not. Block 1 and the table move onto e₁ mostly in the second half of training. By then the anti weight has annealed from about 2.5× the anchor weight to about 0.3×. Holding it at 2× the anchor weight kept both nearly off e₁ on the other ops in deep (E4). So on this pull the recipe anneal lets go too early.

The embedding-lean experiment found the pull on a color embedding to be an order of magnitude or more above what the anti term costs it. In deep the pull reaches the table only through block 1, and a hold of about 7× the recipe value was enough.

The higher hold also took some removal. Some of what the edit removes at the recipe hold seems to sit at block 1. With block 1 kept off e₁, the model answers difference with less of it on e₁.

The anti term also acts on the pulled blocks, and there the alignmentAlignment How far a state points along e₁: the cosine between the state and e₁, which is 1 on the anchor direction and 0 off it. of difference contexts falls a little as the hold rises. So part of the cost may be the hold pressing on the pull itself.

Among the runs here, deep at a hold of 0.2 is the most selective. deep at 0.1 and deep-clean at the recipe hold remove more, with a little more spill.

On the seeds where the answer to difference depends on the e₁ component at block 1 and not on the one at the later blocks (E2), the edit on every slice is the one to keep, and the remaining spill is a question of what block 1 holds.

Four seeds show that deep-clean and the higher holds spill much less than the recipe, but not whether their single runs above the criterion, or the runs with low removal, are typical.
